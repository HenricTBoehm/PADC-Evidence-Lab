**Paper title:** Ungoverned Transduction: When AI-Enabled Effects Outrun Authority

**Author:** Henric T. Böhm

**Date / version:** 27 October 2026; independent Concept Note v0.3.1 (discussion/preprint-style draft; not peer-reviewed).

**Document type:** Conceptual / methodological research note, including a bounded synthetic finite-case test.

**Keywords:** AI governance; ungoverned transduction; PADC; OTAA; authority promotion; workflow authorization; Shadow AI; evidentiary non-evaluability.

**Suggested abstract:** AI capability may reach no observable effect, or may cause an effect without a warranted transition into execution. This note asks whether distinguishing these outcomes adds diagnostic clarity and, separately, whether a reading informed by the earlier Pre-Action Differentiation Cascade (PADC) adds value beyond competent enterprise authorization. We define an effect-window classification, stage-indexed admissibility, and a three-valued guard assessment; then construct a procurement-status countermodel and execute eight deterministic synthetic test cases against an end-to-end authorization baseline. Within that stipulated model, local permissions coexist with a failed global mandate, an admissible effect can have negative value, and missing evidence remains non-evaluable. The same authority-promotion defect is detected by a sufficiently specified baseline, so the test establishes a possible failure pattern but not PADC-specific superiority, novelty or prevalence. A frozen claim inventory, explicit defeat conditions and a prospective comparator protocol specify what further evidence would be required.
